# Global Macro Analysis

Explain the economic regime through growth, inflation, labor, credit, fiscal policy, liquidity, and external balances. Read [regional coverage](references/regions.md) only for the requested countries.

## Build the regime view

Compare release versus consensus, prior observation, and revision separately. Identify seasonality, real versus nominal measures, survey versus hard data, and when data became public. Market expectations require an actual contemporaneous source; otherwise report the change without claiming a surprise.

Contrast the central bank's stated reaction function with the market-implied policy path. For text analysis, compare official statements or transcripts from successive meetings, identify the changed wording in context, handle negation and translation, and separate voting influence from speaking volume. Use direct text evidence before assigning hawkish/dovish categories. Do not assert trained NLP probabilities without a trained and evaluated model.

For China, assess regulatory/political and venue risks before expressing a bullish allocation view. Report documented actions and scenarios separately. Risk scores are rubric-based judgments: explain inputs and scale. A source threshold such as regulatory risk >=4 is a configurable review flag, not authority to cap a real portfolio.

Translate each economic driver to the target asset: hawkish language can support a currency while hurting duration-sensitive equities; easing can indicate deteriorating growth. Macro level and investable surprise differ. Never feed raw hawkish/dovish or inflation scores straight into a bullish/bearish composite.

## Output

Give a regional regime summary, recent evidence with release dates and revisions, priced versus unpriced policy scenarios, asset transmission channels, and top risks. Separate tactical policy/event effects from medium-term cycle and long-term structural conclusions. State unavailable market-implied pricing and avoid replacing it with the policy rate.


## Evidence and signal contract

Record the asset/venue, currency, analysis timestamp, observation period, source links, and publication/availability dates. Refresh current claims from primary sources when permitted; with supplied-data or offline-only requests, label unverified current claims rather than assuming live verification. Identify estimates and unavailable inputs explicitly. Missing evidence is unavailable, not a neutral score. Source examples and portfolio names are illustrations, not current holdings or facts.

When a directional signal helps, give an asset-specific score from -2 (bearish) to +2 (bullish), confidence High/Medium/Low with a reason, horizon, key risk, and falsification condition. Keep tactical (0–4 weeks), positional (1–6 months), and thematic (6–24 months) assessments separate. Confidence is an evidence assessment, not a probability. Omit a score when evidence is inadequate.
