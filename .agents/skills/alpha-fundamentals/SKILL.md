# Fundamental & Sector Analysis

Build a thesis from reported economics to normalized cash flows and valuation. Read [model checks](references/model-checks.md) when building a financial model or investigating accounting quality.

## Company and sector economics

Verify issuer, listing, currency, fiscal period, statement basis, diluted shares and corporate actions. Prioritize filings, audited statements, earnings materials and official sector data. Reconcile revenue, margin, working capital, capital expenditure, debt, dilution and cash conversion. Distinguish reported, adjusted and forecast figures.

Identify sector-specific drivers and peers: banks require NIM, credit costs, capital and funding; airlines require capacity, load factor, yield and hedged fuel exposure; software requires retention, monetization and cash cost of growth; healthcare requires trial, manufacturing, patent and reimbursement evidence. The source's ticker universe illustrates these methods; it does not mandate particular companies or holdings.

## Supply-chain and digital evidence

Map supplier/customer edges with dates, source and certainty. Separate disclosed revenue concentration from inferred dependencies; unknown edges remain unknown. Stress geographic bottlenecks, substitution time, inventory buffers and pass-through. Network centrality is not automatically earnings importance.

For software adoption, distinguish commits, contributors, stars, package downloads, active users, paying customers and revenue. Check bots, mirrors, repeat downloads, telemetry coverage and open-source versus commercial entities. Trace the mechanism from adoption to monetization and costs before treating developer momentum as an investment signal.

## Valuation and thesis

Use a suitable method (FCFF/FCFE DCF, dividend/residual income, asset/NAV, SOTP or comparable multiples). Make growth, reinvestment, discount rate, terminal assumptions and equity bridge explicit. Run bear/base/bull and sensitivity cases rather than reporting a single precise fair value. Explain competitive durability, management capital allocation, sector cycle and what the price implies.

## Output

Give the thesis, material accounting/operating evidence, valuation range and assumptions, peer/sector context, catalysts, risks, and falsification conditions. Mark model outputs as estimates. If historical factor returns or proprietary customer data are unavailable, do not invent factor regressions or exact supply-chain exposure.

Source adaptation: Prompts 12, 13, 15 and 17.

## Evidence and signal contract

Record the asset/venue, currency, analysis timestamp, observation period, source links, and publication/availability dates. Refresh current claims from primary sources when permitted; with supplied-data or offline-only requests, label unverified current claims rather than assuming live verification. Identify estimates and unavailable inputs explicitly. Missing evidence is unavailable, not a neutral score. Source examples and portfolio names are illustrations, not current holdings or facts.

When a directional signal helps, give an asset-specific score from -2 (bearish) to +2 (bullish), confidence High/Medium/Low with a reason, horizon, key risk, and falsification condition. Keep tactical (0–4 weeks), positional (1–6 months), and thematic (6–24 months) assessments separate. Confidence is an evidence assessment, not a probability. Omit a score when evidence is inadequate.
